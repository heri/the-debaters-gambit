## The Debater's Gambit Art Direction: **Ink & Spark**

Think of it as a **debating club that a 9-year-old broke into and redecorated.** The bones are classic and intelligent. The energy is mischievous and alive.

---

### 🎨 Visual Style

**The metaphor:** An old-fashioned debating society — leather chairs, brass lamps, wood panelling — but someone let a kid run wild with luminous ink, gold leaf, and a lot of opinions. Formal structure. Chaotic energy inside it.

**Backgrounds:** Deep ink navy (`#0D0F1C`) as the base — keep the darkness, it's actually right. But warm it with **amber**, **parchment**, and **electric teal** accents rather than purple. Purple reads as generic "game app." Amber reads as brass lamps and wit.

**Cards:** Each card should look like a **real physical object** — thick rounded corners, a subtle paper texture or linen grain, a coloured edge (Bronze = copper, Silver = cool grey, Gold = genuine gold, Easy = warm cream). The card face has an illustrated icon, not an emoji. Think **bold, graphic, slightly heraldic** — the kind of illustration you'd find on a vintage broadside or debate society crest, but with a modern flat-ink feel. Closer to the aesthetic of *Exploding Kittens* or *Taco Cat Goat Cheese Pizza* — smart, hand-crafted feeling, slightly absurd.

**Typography:** Two fonts only.
- **Display (titles, card names):** Something with personality and punch — a condensed slab serif like *Alfa Slab One* or *Playfair Display Black*. It should feel like a newspaper headline from a very opinionated editor.
- **Body (descriptions, rules):** A clean, warm rounded sans — *Nunito* or *Quicksand*. Approachable, readable for kids, but not babyish.

**Colour palette:**
- `#0D0F1C` — Ink Navy (background)
- `#F5C842` — Debate Gold (primary accent, CTA buttons, highlights)
- `#E8DFC8` — Parchment (card background, secondary text)
- `#2ECFB0` — Electric Teal (streak, daily, live indicators)
- `#C0392B` — Argument Red (CON side, timers, danger states)
- `#3B82F6` — Counsel Blue (PRO side)
- `#8B6914` — Bronze (tier 1 cards)

**Animations:** Cards should *physically flip* — not slide. When the Random button is tapped, cards should spin like a roulette wheel and land with a satisfying thud. The reveal moment (both move cards flipping simultaneously) should feel like a magician's reveal. Sound design matters here: a card-slap sound, a crowd murmur, a little flourish.

---

### 🦊 The Mascot: **Argus**

The fox is right. Don't change species. A fox is: clever, slightly cheeky, associated with outsmarting bigger opponents, visually distinctive. It's also cross-cultural and gender-neutral.

**Argus's design:**
- Wears a **tiny barrister's wig** (the white curly kind) but styled cool — slightly tilted, one side flopped. Not silly, just characterful.
- **Waistcoat** in deep teal with a gold pocket watch chain. He looks like he belongs in a debating society but also like he'd win a street argument.
- **Expression range** is his superpower: you need at minimum — Confident, Suspicious (squinting, The Magnifying Glass), Dramatic (gasping, for big moves), Smug (for wins), Encouraging (for after a loss), Thinking (Solo Dojo mode).
- **Style:** Flat vector with thick outlines and limited colour fills — works at any size, reads clearly on dark backgrounds, scalable from 16px favicon to full splash screen.
- **Voice/tone when he speaks:** Short, punchy, a little theatrical. Never talks down. Treats the kid like a worthy opponent. *"Interesting move. Let's see if it lands."* *"Your opponent thinks they've got you. Do they?"*

---

### 🎮 Tone of Voice

This is where it really separates from Duolingo. Duolingo is encouraging and gentle. The Debater's Gambit should feel like a **sparring partner who respects you.**

| Situation | Duolingo tone | Debater's Gambit tone |
|---|---|---|
| After a win | "Amazing! You're on fire! 🔥" | "Verdict: Decisive. Argus approves." |
| After a loss | "Don't give up! Try again!" | "A strong opponent. Study their move. Come back sharper." |
| Daily prompt | "Time to practice! 🌟" | "Today's case is on the board. Are you ready to argue?" |
| Streak | "You're on a 7-day streak!" | "7 days undefeated. The Parlour remembers." |
| Unlocking a card | "New achievement!" | "You've earned this. The [Card Name] is yours." |

The game treats kids as **junior debaters**, not students. That's a crucial distinction. They're not learning — they're practising. They're not pupils — they're members of the Parlour of Minds (which is already in your original document and is a brilliant phrase you should lean into hard).

---

### 📱 App Icon

**Concept:** Argus's face — just the face, confident smirk, wig slightly tilted — inside a **shield shape** that has the feel of a debating society crest. The shield background is Ink Navy. A single gold laurel detail on either side. No text on the icon.

At small sizes (the actual icon on a phone screen) it reads as: clever fox face on a dark badge. Immediately distinctive in an app drawer full of colourful rounded squares.

**Alt concept** if you want to go more abstract: A **golden speech bubble** containing two crossed quills — like crossed swords, but for words. Clean, iconic, scalable. Argus appears inside the app but not on the icon.

Recommendation: go with Argus on the icon. Mascots that appear on icons create brand recognition faster. Duo the owl on the Duolingo icon is half the reason people remember the app exists when they scroll past it.

### What to Change in the Current Iteration specifically

The current app is closer than it looks - the dark background and gold typography are right. The fixes:

- In the directory, there are various png images. These are images of Argus, the app mascot that must be used as app icons and inside the app.
- **Change the purple card border** to a warm amber/gold glow — purple is the one colour fighting the palette.
- **CHOOSE A CASE** headline is good — keep the condensed caps, but use the slab serif rather than whatever rounded sans is there now.
- **PREV / NEXT** navigation should feel more like turning pages — a paper-fold micro-animation would be perfect here.
- **The card counter (1/17)** should become dots styled as wax seals — tiny filled/unfilled circles with more character than plain dots.
- **CHOOSE THIS and RANDOM buttons** are well-placed — just restyle RANDOM to feel more theatrical. A spinning animation on tap, "Argus chooses..." text while it spins, then the card lands.

---

The north star for every design decision: **it should look like a place a clever kid wants to belong to.** Not a classroom. Not a toy. The Parlour of Minds.
