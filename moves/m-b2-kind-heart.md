## STEP 1 — Understand Kindness

### S1.1 — INFO
*Argus: confident*
**Heading:** What is Kindness?
**Body:** Kindness means caring about how other people feel — and choosing to help them, even when you don't have to. It's not just being nice. It's noticing someone needs something and doing something about it.

---

### S1.2 — INFO
*Argus: confident*
**Heading:** Kindness in Action
**Body:** A mum baked cookies for her two children. She spent all afternoon making them. When they were ready, she wanted to make sure both children felt equally loved — so she thought carefully about how to share them fairly and warmly.

---

### S1.3 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** A mother has 3 cookies for her 2 children. What should she do?

**A)** Give 2 cookies to one child and 1 to the other.
→ *wrong* — "That would leave one child with much less than the other. Kindness means thinking about how each person feels — and one of them would feel left out."

**B)** Give 1 cookie to each child and keep 1 for herself.
→ *correct* — "That's right! Everyone gets something, and nobody feels left out. That's what kindness looks like — everyone gets to enjoy it together."

**C)** Eat all the cookies herself because she baked them.
→ *wrong* — "She did work hard! But kindness means thinking about others, not just yourself. The children were expecting some too."

---

### S1.4 — INFO
*Argus: confident*
**Heading:** Bigger Kindness
**Body:** Some people give up their free weekend time to volunteer at a local shelter. They help cook meals and sit with people who are lonely. Nobody asks them to. Nobody pays them. They do it because kindness is a choice they make.

---

### S1.5 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** Why does someone volunteer at a shelter on their day off?

**A)** To get something in return.
→ *wrong* — "Kindness that expects something back is more like a trade. True kindness doesn't come with a bill."

**B)** Because they were told to by their school or work.
→ *wrong* — "That might be why some people start volunteering. But the ones who keep going, week after week, do it for a different reason."

**C)** Because they care about making things better for someone else, even when it's hard.
→ *correct* — "Exactly! That's the heart of kindness — choosing to help, not because you gain something, but because someone else needs it."

## STEP 2 — Understand the Feeling

### S2.1 — INFO
*Argus: encouraging*
**Quote (speech bubble):** "I am so angry! Nobody understands me. I don't want to play with you."
**Argus note:** Something is very wrong here. Let's figure out what this person is actually feeling before we react.

---

### S2.2 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** Why is this person angry?

**A)** They want to play alone.
→ *wrong* — "Look at the words again: 'Nobody understands me.' That's not about wanting to be alone — it's about something else entirely."

**B)** They feel misunderstood — like nobody is really listening to them.
→ *correct* — "Exactly. The anger is on the surface, but underneath it is a need to feel heard. That's what The Kind Heart looks for first."

**C)** They are just having a bad day.
→ *wrong* — "That might be true — but 'bad day' doesn't tell you how to respond. 'Feeling misunderstood' does. That's why we look for the real feeling."

---

### S2.3 — INFO
*Argus: encouraging*
**Quote (speech bubble):** "Why did you guys leave me alone in the park! I don't want to talk to you anymore."
**Argus note:** This one is more complicated. They're upset — but about what, exactly? There might be more than one feeling here.

---

### S2.4 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** What feelings does this person have?

**A)** Only angry.
→ *wrong* — "Anger is definitely there. But look at what happened — they were LEFT alone. Is anger the only thing you'd feel if that happened to you?"

**B)** Only sad.
→ *wrong* — "Sadness is there too. But they're also shouting 'Why did you leave me!' That's not just sadness — there's something else."

**C)** Both angry AND sad — they feel abandoned and hurt.
→ *correct* — "Right! They're angry because it happened, and sad because it hurt. When someone has two big feelings at once, they really need to feel understood before anything else. That's exactly what The Kind Heart does."

## STEP 3 — See It in Arguments

### S3.1 — INFO
*Argus: thinking*
**Heading:** How The Kind Heart Works in Arguments
**Body:** When you use The Kind Heart, you don't start with your argument. You start by showing that you understand how the other person feels. Once they feel heard, they're much more open to hearing your point. It's not weakness — it's strategy.

---

### S3.2 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Context:** *"I know you're a kind person, and I think your help would mean a lot to the team right now."*
**Prompt:** What is this person doing?

**A)** Making a logical argument about why help is needed.
→ *wrong* — "They're not making a list of reasons. They're doing something else — they're speaking to who the other person is, not just what they need."

**B)** Appealing to the other person's kindness to encourage them to act.
→ *correct* — "Yes! They reminded the other person that they are kind — and that framing makes it much harder to say no. They didn't argue facts. They reminded someone of who they are."

**C)** Threatening them if they don't help.
→ *wrong* — "The opposite! This is the softest kind of persuasion — it works because it makes the other person want to help, not because it forces them."

---

### S3.3 — INFO
*Argus: thinking*
**Heading:** The Kind Heart in a Real Debate
**Body:** In a class debate about school lunches, a student said: *"By making sure every kid has a hot meal, we're showing kindness to the children who need it most. That's the kind of school we want to be — one where we look out for each other."*
**Argus note:** Notice: they didn't argue about food costs or nutrition data. They made it about what kind of people we are. That's The Kind Heart at its most powerful.

---

### S3.4 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** Why does using kindness in an argument make it stronger?

**A)** Because it makes the other side feel guilty.
→ *wrong* — "Guilt can shut people down — it doesn't open them up. The Kind Heart works the other way: it makes people want to do the kind thing."

**B)** Because it connects the argument to something people already care about: being a good person.
→ *correct* — "Exactly! Most people want to see themselves as kind. When you show that the kind choice is YOUR choice, you make it easy for them to agree with you."

**C)** Because the other side will be too emotional to argue back.
→ *wrong* — "The goal isn't to overwhelm them emotionally — it's to connect with them. There's a big difference."

## STEP 4 — Put It Together

### S4.1 — INFO (Full Technique Demonstration)
*Argus: thinking*
**Opponent says:** "We should cut the school library budget. Nobody really uses it anymore."
**Response:** "I understand why it might seem that way — if you don't use it much yourself, it can feel like nobody does. But for kids who don't have books at home, that library is the only quiet place they have to read. Keeping it open is one of the kindest things our school can do for the students who need it most."
**Argus note:** Notice how the response STARTS with acknowledging the opponent's view ('I understand why it might seem that way'). Only then does it pivot to the argument. That's The Kind Heart.

---

### S4.2 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Opponent says:** "I am NOT happy. We should have NO homework EVER because kids are completely exhausted after school."
**Prompt:** Which response uses The Kind Heart correctly?

**A)** "What's wrong with homework? It's actually good for you."
→ *wrong* — "That ignores their feeling completely. The Kind Heart always acknowledges the feeling first — then argues. Jumping straight to 'you're wrong' will just make them angrier."

**B)** "I actually love homework, so I disagree."
→ *wrong* — "Making it about yourself doesn't help. The Kind Heart is about showing you understand THEIR experience — not broadcasting yours."

**C)** "I know — I get tired too. And doing just a little homework keeps what we learned fresh so we do better tomorrow. Maybe less homework, not none?"
→ *correct* — "Perfect! You started with 'I know — I get tired too.' That's the acknowledgement. Then you gently pivoted to your argument. That's The Kind Heart."

---

### S4.3 — INFO (Harder Scenario)
*Argus: thinking*
**Argus note:** This one has two feelings underneath. Make sure your response catches both.
**Opponent says:** "I've been trying to show everyone my science project for THREE days and every time something else comes first. I'm starting to think nobody even cares what I've been working on."

---

### S4.4 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** Which response uses The Kind Heart correctly for this harder situation?

**A)** "The schedule has been really busy. It's not personal."
→ *wrong* — "That explains the situation but dismisses the feeling. 'It's not personal' is one of the least comforting things you can say to someone who feels invisible."

**B)** "I can see you're frustrated — and honestly, you should be. Three days is a long time to wait. Your project matters and I think we should make it the first thing we do tomorrow."
→ *correct* — "That catches both feelings: the frustration AND the sense of invisibility. Then it pivots to a concrete, kind action. That's The Kind Heart at full strength."

**C)** "Your project is probably really good, don't worry."
→ *wrong* — "That's encouraging, but it sidesteps the real feeling — that they feel forgotten. The Kind Heart names the feeling before offering comfort."

## STEP 5 — Say It

### S5.1 — INFO (Model Response)
*Argus: encouraging*
**Heading:** The Kind Heart — Out Loud
**Body:**
> "I understand why you feel that way — if I were in your position, I might feel the same. AND I think there's a kinder way through this that works for both of us. Here's what I'd suggest."

**Argus caption:** Read this to yourself first. Three parts: acknowledge → show you genuinely understand → gently steer. Short. Warm. Steady.
