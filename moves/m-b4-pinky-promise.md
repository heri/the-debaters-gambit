## STEP 1 — Understand Commitment

### S1.1 — INFO
*Argus: confident*
**Heading:** What Makes a Promise Real?
**Body:** A real promise isn't "maybe" or "I'll try." A real promise is specific — it says exactly what will happen, when, and how. The more specific a promise is, the more you trust the person making it. Vague promises sound like excuses waiting to happen.

---

### S1.2 — INFO
*Argus: confident*
**Heading:** The Vague Promise vs. The Real Promise
**Body:** Friend A says: *"I'll probably come to your birthday. I might be able to make it."* Friend B says: *"I'll be there at 3pm with the blue-wrapped present, and I'm bringing your favourite crisps."* Which friend do you trust more?

---

### S1.3 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** Why does Friend B sound more trustworthy?

**A)** Because they have a better present.
→ *wrong* — "The present isn't what makes the promise trustworthy. Look at the structure of what they said — the detail, the specifics."

**B)** Because they gave exact details — time, gift, food — which shows they've actually thought it through.
→ *correct* — "Exactly! Specific details prove that someone has committed with their mind, not just their mouth. Vague promises can always be wriggled out of. Specific ones can't."

**C)** Because they seem more excited.
→ *wrong* — "Excitement fades. Commitment is about what you DO — and the specifics show what they're actually planning to do."

---

### S1.4 — INFO
*Argus: confident*
**Heading:** Commitment in the Bigger World
**Body:** When a builder quotes a job, they don't say "it'll probably be fine and should cost roughly something." They say: "I'll finish by Friday the 14th, it will cost £240, and here's exactly what I'll fix." That level of detail is how you know they mean it.

---

### S1.5 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** Why does giving specific details make people trust you more?

**A)** Because it shows you've actually thought about what you're promising — not just saying words.
→ *correct* — "Right! Detail is the proof of real commitment. Anyone can say 'I promise.' Only someone who means it can say exactly what, when, and how."

**B)** Because long answers always sound smarter.
→ *wrong* — "Length alone doesn't build trust. A specific short promise ('I'll be there at 3pm') is more trustworthy than a long vague one ('I'll do my best to come sometime in the afternoon, probably')."

**C)** Because it gives the other person more to argue about.
→ *wrong* — "The goal of The Pinky Promise is certainty — not to invite more debate. Specifics close arguments down, not open them up."

## STEP 3 — See It in Arguments

### S3.1 — INFO
*Argus: thinking*
**Heading:** How The Pinky Promise Works in Arguments
**Body:** In a debate, vague arguments sound like: "This might work" or "Things would probably be better." The Pinky Promise replaces all of that with certainty. You don't say what might happen — you say exactly what WILL happen, with specific details that show you've thought it through.

---

### S3.2 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Context:** In a debate about getting a class pet, a student said: *"If we get the hamster, here's exactly what will happen: I will clean the cage every Monday. Priya will bring fresh food every Wednesday. By the end of term, we will have a pet AND we will have proved we can be responsible for something living."*
**Prompt:** What makes this argument convincing?

**A)** They mentioned three different people.
→ *wrong* — "The number of people isn't what makes it convincing. Look at the TYPE of things they said — days, actions, outcomes."

**B)** They gave specific, committed details — days, names, a clear outcome — leaving no room for doubt.
→ *correct* — "Yes! Specific details ('Monday', 'Wednesday', 'Priya') turn a wish into a plan. A plan is much harder to argue against than a hope."

**C)** They were very enthusiastic.
→ *wrong* — "Enthusiasm without specifics sounds desperate. Specifics without enthusiasm still sound solid. It's the detail that does the work."

---

### S3.3 — INFO
*Argus: thinking*
**Heading:** The Pinky Promise in a Real Debate
**Body:** In a student council debate, a candidate said: *"If you vote for me, here's exactly what I'll do: first week, I'll run a survey of every class. Second week, I'll present the results to the head teacher. By half-term, we'll have a concrete proposal on the table. Not promises — a plan."*
**Argus note:** Notice how they replaced "I'll try to make things better" with a timeline and specific actions. That's the difference between hope and commitment.

---

### S3.4 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** Why does a specific commitment persuade people better than a general promise?

**A)** Because it sounds more impressive.
→ *wrong* — "It's not about sounding impressive — it's about being believable. Specific details are evidence that you've actually thought it through."

**B)** Because it shows you've planned it — which means you believe it will actually work.
→ *correct* — "Exactly! If you're willing to say exactly what will happen, it's because you're genuinely confident it will. That confidence is contagious."

**C)** Because the other side won't have time to check your facts.
→ *wrong* — "The goal isn't to bluff faster than they can check. The Pinky Promise works because the specifics are REAL — they're a genuine commitment."

## STEP 4 — Put It Together

### S4.1 — INFO (Full Technique Demonstration)
*Argus: thinking*
**Opponent says:** "Kids can't be trusted to look after the school garden. They'll forget and everything will die."
**Response:** "Here's exactly what will happen: Group A waters on Monday and Thursday. Group B weeds on Wednesday. I will check every Friday and if something is wrong, I will fix it that day. By the end of term, the garden will be healthier than it is now. I'll stake my end-of-term grade on it."
**Argus note:** Notice: no "hopefully" or "we'll try." Every sentence is a specific commitment. That's The Pinky Promise — your certainty IS the argument.

---

### S4.2 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Opponent says:** "Longer playtime is a nice idea, but it'll probably just lead to more mess and more arguments. It's not worth the trouble."
**Prompt:** Which response uses The Pinky Promise correctly?

**A)** "Longer playtime would be great for everyone. Trust us."
→ *wrong* — "'Trust us' without specifics is exactly the kind of vague promise The Pinky Promise replaces. What will you actually do?"

**B)** "It might work out fine, and I think we should try it."
→ *wrong* — "'Might' and 'think' are the enemy. The Pinky Promise removes all the maybes."

**C)** "Here's what will happen: the two duty teachers will be on the field every day, we'll add one extra litter bin, and I'll personally write a report after two weeks showing whether it's working. If it's not, we stop. Deal?"
→ *correct* — "That's The Pinky Promise! Specific people, specific actions, a clear timeline, and a direct challenge to the opponent. Certainty wins."

---

### S4.3 — INFO (Harder Scenario)
*Argus: thinking*
**Argus note:** This one is trickier. The opponent isn't just skeptical — they have a real, specific worry. Your commitment needs to match their specific concern.
**Opponent says:** "I'm worried that if we stay up an extra half hour, we'll be too tired the next morning and do worse at school. That's not worth it."

---

### S4.4 — QUESTION
*Argus: thinking*
**Prompt:** What's the best response using The Pinky Promise?

**A)** "We won't be tired. Kids can handle staying up later."
→ *wrong* — "That's a blanket denial — it doesn't address their specific worry or give them any reason to believe you."

**B)** "I'll use that extra half hour to read, not screens. I'll be in bed by 9:15 with the light off. If my grades drop in the next month, we go back to 8:45. You can check."
→ *correct* — "That's The Pinky Promise at full strength! You matched their specific worry (tiredness → school performance) with specific commitments (reading, exact bedtime, a measurable test period, and accountability). Unbeatable."

**C)** "Lots of kids stay up later and they're fine."
→ *wrong* — "That's an appeal to others, not a personal commitment. The Pinky Promise is about YOUR specific plan — not what other people do."
